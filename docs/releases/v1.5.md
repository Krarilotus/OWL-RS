# NRESE 1.5 (prerelease)

NRESE is an RDF store with built-in OWL reasoning, written in Rust: a hobby project, developed in the open. This
prerelease is the work of the `refactor/engine-v2` branch since `main`. Its tag is `v1.5.0-pre.1`, and the
crates' package version in `Cargo.toml` is 1.5.0.

The 1.5 line is for our own applications, and for anyone curious enough to try it. The first release meant for
other people's use is 2.0.

This page has what is particular to this release and its measurements. Limits that outlive it are in
[limits and deviations](../limits.md). Every figure on this page was measured on an earlier tree of this release,
each on one machine, and none was measured again on the tree this page comes with; where the tree is named, it is
that one.

## What is new since `main`

- **OWL 2 DL.** A new `owl2-dl` mode: OWL 2 RL as a lower bound, plus a hypertableau and a consequence-based engine
  for consistency on commit, classification, realisation and certain answers where they can be proven.
- **Every answer says what it promises.** A `NRESE-Completeness` header marks answers as complete, sound with rows
  possibly missing (and why), or unsound or not certified where neither holds. Without reasoning, or with
  `infer=false`, it says `complete`. With reasoning on, a read inside a transaction with operations pending is never
  `complete`; with nothing pending it is answered as outside one. An answer from the result cache carries the status
  its first answer was given.
- **Read replicas** that follow a primary's log. A replica serves its primary's statements without its primary's
  reasoning certificate: its reasoned reads say `not-certified`, under `dl.answers = "exact"` they are refused, and
  its consistency reads `unknown` ([replication](../ops/replication.md#limits-of-this-first-step)). Start a replica
  from an empty data directory: it then takes an image from the primary. Over a directory that holds a store it
  takes none and logs no warning, and serves what that directory holds.
- **Operations.** A clean stop on SIGTERM or ctrl-c within 8 s; a memory budget for the whole process and for each
  request, so an operation past it fails and the process doesn't; the routes of the earlier release still answer,
  marked deprecated, until the next release.
- **Bulk loads keep what they can't read in a quarantine,** per repository, to fix and re-admit or discard.
  `--strict` stops at the first bad statement, as before.
- **A start whose rebuild of the inferences fails on a defect** (at startup, or after a bulk load) ends with a
  message that names the data directory and says how to start without reasoning: `reasoner.mode = "disabled"` or
  `NRESE_REASONING_MODE=disabled`.
- **Classification through the endpoint** (`GET /dataset/classification`). On 23 OWL 2 EL inputs, its answer
  equals the reference on 22, and `main`'s on 19: `main` leaves out the classes equivalent to `owl:Thing` on four
  ORE tasks, because its answer has no field for them. It is faster wherever `main` takes more than about 10 ms
  (down to 0.03 times `main`'s time), and 1 to 5 ms slower on the smallest inputs. Each server process pays about
  1.3 ms once, at its first classification; after that, the smallest task takes about 0.57 ms against `main`'s
  0.15. Measured at `f3083f146`. Its memory: [trade-offs](#trade-offs-accepted-for-this-release).
- **Answers outside OWL 2 DL.** On inputs outside OWL 2 DL, NRESE now answers by default where it has a proven
  procedure. Today that is classification of an ontology with an irregular role hierarchy, where that is all the
  DL engines leave out. Every subsumption listed holds under the OWL 2 Direct Semantics, and none is missing unless
  the input states an object-property range; with a range, the answer says it may be incomplete. Such answers say
  so in the `NRESE-Completeness` header, the JSON and the N-Triples form. Reasoners that check OWL 2 DL's
  restrictions refuse these inputs. [`dl.outside_profile`](../ops/config-reference.md#dl.outside_profile)` =
  dl-part`, on the server or per repository, restores the earlier answer: the DL engines' part, marked incomplete.
  On the irregular-hierarchy case it was measured on, it takes 0.56 s against `main`'s 4.9 s, with equal
  taxonomies, and peaks at 754 against 265 MiB, the load included.
- **User rules that say what OWL 2 RL's equality rules say are reasoned as equality, whatever their names.** A
  Notation3 or `.pie` program that holds the five rules over `owl:sameAs` (symmetry, transitivity, replacement in
  the subject, predicate and object) uses the equality module, with the same answers. A rule is known by what it
  says: one named like an equality rule that says something else is an ordinary rule.
- **Under `reasoner.unnamed_classes = "skip"`, whether a hidden class's members are stored as `owl:Thing`s is asked
  of the rules, not read from a rule's name.** Where the rules make a declared class's members things, as
  `owl2-rl`'s do, they are; where they don't, those memberships are left out with nothing in their place.
- **The search scope as a setting:** `store.search_scope = "reader"` ranks text and vectors from what the reader may
  read. It is slower for filtered vector search, about 1.5 times. The default, `global`, is unchanged.
- **RDF4J's older media type names** for N-Quads and TriG are accepted.
- **Jena's and rdflib's names for the default graph** (`urn:x-arq:DefaultGraph`, `urn:x-rdflib:default`) name the
  default graph in updates (`CLEAR`, `DROP`, `CREATE`, `COPY`, `MOVE`) and in RDF4J's statements. To `main` they are
  ordinary named graphs.
- **A `COUNT` that reaches 2^64** fails with a range error that says so.

The full list, with the limits next to each item: [features and standards](../what-it-covers.md) and
[limits and deviations](../limits.md).

### Fixed: wrong answers and a crash that `main` gives

- **Classification on four input shapes.** The shapes: an annotation property given an `rdfs:domain` or an
  `rdfs:range`, and datatypes declared as `owl:DataRange`, each plain and with an irregular role chain. `main`
  answers all four wrong: it names properties and datatypes as classes and lists subsumptions the input doesn't
  state, and it marks none of that (no completeness header; the JSON reports "OWL 2 EL" with nothing skipped).
  - By default (`proven`), three of the four answers are right and complete. The annotation shape with the chain is
    answered by the EL route and marked as outside OWL 2 DL. The data-range shape with the chain gets the DL
    engines' sound part, marked sound-only with its reason.
  - Under `dl-part`, the two plain shapes get the same answer as by default. The two shapes with the chain get the
    DL engines' sound part, marked sound-only with the reason, and each misses three subsumptions.
- **Classification with a property declared on a blank node.** `main` drops that property's axioms without a word,
  however it is typed. This release also reads a sub-property, domain or range that involves an annotation property
  (`rdfs:label`, say) as an annotation axiom, which says nothing about classes.
- **`LIMIT 0` over several patterns no longer crashes.** On `main`, with a first pattern larger than one batch of
  1,024 rows, the query panics after the response has begun, so the client gets a 200 whose body ends right after
  the headers, and the server stays up. Now it answers 200 with the query's variables and no rows.
- **`COPY`, `MOVE` and `ADD` copy the stored source graph only.** On `main`, the union default graph copied every
  graph, the inferred statements were copied as data, and `using-graph-uri` changed which graph was copied.
- **`sameTerm` with a pre-bound blank node** matches it, and an `OPTIONAL` with `DISTINCT` over asserted data no
  longer joins `owl:sameAs` identities.
- **A signed number after a path element** (`?s :p+3`) is the object `+3`, as SPARQL's longest-token rule says.
- **Under `reasoner.equality = "compact"`, with a program of custom rules alone that holds the equality rules,**
  `main` answers too few rows: members of an `owl:sameAs` class lose inferred statements, as written when a class's
  type comes before its `owl:sameAs`, and after a rematerialisation in any order. To repair a store on `main`: set
  `reasoner.equality = "representatives"` and restart.
- **A reader allowed only some graphs who sees inferred statements** no longer loses an inferred statement when a
  graph it may not read asserts that same statement.

What a reader of only some graphs could learn on `main`, now closed:
- **A text search under `GRAPH ?g`** read every named graph, and `FROM NAMED` didn't restrict it.
- **A login that overlapped a change of its password** could leave a session with the old password valid after the
  change. A session opened before the change already ended with it.
- **A workspace or user name used again** inherited the removed one's graphs and saved queries. It now starts empty.

## Upgrading

A data directory written by `main` opens as it is, with no migration step; with reasoning on, the inferences are
rebuilt once at the first start, before the server reports ready ([upgrading](../ops/server-setup.md#upgrading)).
In one run on one machine, under `owl2-rl` on disk, that start was ready after 0.22 s at 100,837 statements and
0.34 s at 1,272,869. An image backup `main` wrote restores under this release.

Keep a copy of the data directory before you upgrade. `main` opens the directory again until this release writes
its first checkpoint (a bulk load, a background or an explicit checkpoint). Checkpoints are now written in format
12, and after the first one `main` stops with "… uses an unsupported storage format; reload the data into a new directory". Its advice to reload isn't the way
back: the copy is, opened with `main`'s binary.

Nothing is promised yet about the storage format. A later 1.x release may change it again, each time with a one-way
upgrade from the release before it. How far back a release reads is promised from 2.0 on.

What else changes for a user of `main`:
- **Under `owl2-rl` and `owl2-dl`, a literal outside its datatype under a range is an inconsistency**
  (`"abc"^^xsd:integer` where the range is `xsd:integer`, or any literal typed `rdfs:Literal`), which `main`
  accepted. A commit that adds one is refused, and a store from `main` that holds one starts in quarantine:
  `/readyz` answers 503 `quarantined`, and the info says `inconsistent` with the number of violations. Reads are
  still answered, `sound-only`, and so are writes. Nothing names the statement: find such literals and delete them,
  before the upgrade or after; the store is then ready again, and stays so across restarts
  ([upgrading](../ops/server-setup.md#upgrading)).
- **Under `owl2-ql`, answers come through existentials.** The QL rewriting is on by default there, so a query can
  find individuals that only an existential axiom makes match. `reasoner.ql_rewriting = "off"` keeps the earlier
  answers.
- **`/readyz` answers only its status** (`ready`, `starting`, `quarantined`). Its figures (counts, revision) are in
  `GET /api/v1/repositories/{id}/info`, for a reader of every graph or an administrator.
- **A rule named `asserted` or `axiom` is refused** (an `Id:` in a `.pie` ruleset): the message names the rule and
  says the name is reserved for a proof's own steps. A repository whose stored rules have one is listed as
  unavailable, with that reason, until the rule is renamed in the repository's `repository.json` and the server
  restarted. A server whose configured rule file has one stops at its start with the message.
- **A rejected commit says what kind of reason it is shown.** A commit the SHACL gate rejects answers 400 with the
  problem type `https://nrese.dev/problems/shacl-reject` ("SHACL Reject"), no longer `reasoner-reject`. A commit
  that makes the data inconsistent still answers `reasoner-reject`. A committer that may read only some graphs is
  told the reason its own graphs show; if they show none, it gets `https://nrese.dev/problems/commit-reject`
  ("Commit Reject") for every check, so the label can't hint at graphs it may not read. The problem's
  `reasoner_reject` field keeps its name. For Rust users of the server crate: `ApiError::ReasonerReject` is now
  `ApiError::Rejected`, with a `kind`, and the store's `MutationReject` has a field `kind: RejectKind`.
- **One-shot commands log to standard error,** so `nrese-server query --format json` writes only the JSON to
  standard output. The server itself still logs to standard output.
- **The OpenAPI description's operation ids change:** `query` becomes `query_get` and `query_post`, `sparql`
  becomes `sparql_get` and `sparql_post`. A client generated from the description changes its method names; the
  wire doesn't change.
- **The N-Quads export's checksum moves to the end:** a closing comment, and over HTTP the trailer
  `x-nrese-checksum-sha256` ([N-Quads exports](../ops/backups.md#n-quads-exports)).
- **New repository ids** can't start or end with `.` and can't be a Windows device name. Existing repositories keep
  working.
- **A file whose content doesn't match its extension** (N-Quads named `.nt`) loads in the default quarantine mode:
  the statements it can't read are quarantined, not loaded, and the load warns with their count
  ([bulk loading](../ops/server-setup.md#loading-data)).

## Trade-offs accepted for this release

- **Memory at a large bulk load.** Loading DBpedia core (67 M statements) into an on-disk store peaks at 5.95 GiB,
  against 5.20 GiB in `main`, and it finishes 1.8 times as fast (15.4 s against 28.0 s). Two settings lower the
  peak, each at a cost in load time:
  - [`budgets.bulk_load_memory`](../ops/config-reference.md#budgets.bulk_load_memory)` = "1GiB"` sorts the load's
    statements in chunks on disk. The peak is 4.58 GiB, below `main`'s, and the load takes 26.8 s, about `main`'s
    time. The default is 25 % of the machine's memory, or of the container's limit.
  - [`budgets.huge_pages`](../ops/config-reference.md#budgets.huge_pages)` = "off"` keeps transparent huge pages
    off for the whole process. The peak is 5.64 GiB and the load is 14 % slower. It also slows reasoning over large
    data (LUBM 100's materialisation, by about a quarter). `"bulk"` keeps huge pages on during a load, so it doesn't
    lower this peak.
- **EL classification's memory against its time.** Classifying an OWL 2 EL ontology through the endpoint is about
  ten times as fast as in `main` (0.52 s against 5.4 s for 20,000 classes) and uses about 3.5 times its memory at
  its peak (673 MiB against 194 MiB, the load included).
  [`dl.memory`](../ops/config-reference.md#dl.memory) caps it, but a classification that reaches the cap answers
  incomplete instead of using less. `budgets.huge_pages` is the other setting that bears on it; it wasn't measured
  on this case. How the trade-off changes with the ontology's size wasn't measured either. Against the v1.5
  baseline (engine-v2 at `d348a8a1`, 9 October) the peak is unchanged (0.993, 95 % interval 0.976 to 1.011) and
  the time too (0.987, 0.978 to 0.997). That closes the peak against the baseline, not the memory the process
  keeps resident after the request.
- **A query can be refused while part of what it reserved is unused** ([memory](../limits.md#memory-and-stopping)).
  Exact charging, the alternative, cost 2.4 % of throughput and 6 to 8 % of the 99th-percentile latency.

## Memory limits

> Memory limits constrain charged query reservations. Some operator working state and lazily built engine
> structures remain outside those reservations; total allocation and resident memory can exceed the configured
> limits. No fixed multiplier bounds that excess.

What a limit counts, what stays outside it and the settings: [limits and deviations](../limits.md#memory-and-stopping).
A design that bounds the rest follows this release.

### A `LIMIT` over several patterns

- While such a `LIMIT` runs, the memory budget counts the first pattern's table, so no other request is admitted
  into room still held. Measured before that fix and after: 162,258 and 642,437 bytes held outside the budget at
  10,000 and 40,000 first-pattern rows, then 2,258 and 2,437.
- After the answer, the budget doesn't keep the first table's charge until the query ends (143,616 bytes in the
  test, before that fix).
- Not fixed: a join that fails inside the loop keeps the charges of inputs already handed to it until the query
  ends. The budget then reads high, and the query fails either way.

### The cyclic counts, against `main`

In this release a memory limit doesn't bound a cyclic count's working state, while `main`'s limit refuses those
counts at small limits. The same queries on both, on one host: engine-v2 at `bda326e1d` and `main` at `a0020134`,
limits doubling from 4 KiB to 256 MiB, the same answers on both.

| query (`COUNT`) | `main`: peak (B) | `main` answers from | engine-v2: peak (B) | engine-v2 answers from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| triangles, skewed, dense ×1 / ×4 | 210,588 / 615,484 | 128 KiB | 594,829 / 957,421 | 4 KiB |
| triangles, uniform, dense ×1 / ×4 | 211,516 / 606,012 | 128 KiB | 395,229 / 795,477 | 4 KiB |
| four-cycles, social ×1 / ×4 | 638,575 / 1,952,367 | 512 KiB / 2 MiB | 418,937 / 618,945 | 4 KiB |
| triangles, social ×1 / ×4 | 408,779 / 1,576,139 | 512 KiB / 2 MiB | 282,437 / 487,549 | 4 KiB |

- **Peak** is the most bytes live at once during the query, on any thread of the process, less what was live
  before it, taken from the run without a limit. It isn't resident memory. **Answers from** is the lowest limit
  under which the query answered.
- Neither figure bounds allocated memory: `main`'s peak at ×1 (210 KB) is above the 128 KiB it first answered
  under.
- engine-v2's peak is below `main`'s on the social data and above it on the dense data.

### Observations: what single queries allocate beyond their charge

One query's evaluation, its results consumed, each in a process of its own, on engine-v2 at `6c212fb56` with a
counting allocator. The store was loaded and the query parsed before the measurement started. Each query ran once
on its store, so a structure built at its first use (the text index, the text order) counts. **Excess** is the
most that the bytes allocated in the window, less the request budget's charges, rose to at any moment. **Charged
peak** is the request budget's highest charge. The limit was 4 GiB, never approached. Data: the benchmark
generators at two sizes, ×1 and ×4.

| query | what most of the excess is | excess ×1 → ×4 (B) | charged peak ×1 → ×4 (B) | on `main` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| triangles, skewed (dense, 1,000 → 4,000 nodes) | the cyclic join's lists, count forest and cursors | 591,869 → 960,469 | 136 → 136 | the forest is new; the lists and cursors are on `main` |
| four-cycles (social, 1,000 → 4,000 people) | the same | 420,737 → 621,049 | 136 → 136 | the same |
| `CONTAINS` (social, 5,000 → 20,000) | its view of the dictionary's keys | 63,084 → 9,748 | 2,640 → 10,320 | `main` runs it without this view |
| `STRSTARTS` | the text order, built in the query and kept | 741,465 → 2,361,961 | 2,640 → 41,056 | yes |
| `ORDER BY … LIMIT` | the decoded sort terms | 808,500 → 2,241,573 | 240,064 → 960,064 | yes |
| `COUNT(DISTINCT …)` per group | the aggregates' members, group ids, value sets | 399,336 → 1,594,920 | 165,633 → 645,634 | yes |
| `OPTIONAL` with a count | the `OPTIONAL`'s key set | 744,267 → 2,950,035 | 1,079,488 → 3,513,736 | yes |
| a star over four graphs, counted | a join's output before it is charged | 363,264 → 1,442,400 | 553,280 → 2,212,928 | yes |
| a join under `LIMIT` | a join's output, and the planner's distinct-value table (1.44 MB, charged to the engine's budget, not the request's) | 2,665,711 → 2,581,315 | 1,112,984 → 1,625,176 | the join's output yes; the table is kept differently on `main` |
| `MINUS` / `NOT EXISTS` | the anti-join's table | 93,912 → 372,808 | 258,368 → 1,033,280 | yes |
| text search (10,000 → 40,000 documents) | the full-text index, built in the first query and kept | 2,580,122 → 29,066,727 | 232,984 → 921,184 | yes |

What these figures don't show:
- **No bound.** Each query was measured on one generator at two sizes. Literal length, distinctness, skew, query
  shape and concurrent operators change how much state a charged byte stands for.
- **No exact sizes per structure.** "What most of the excess is" names the code at the excess's highest points;
  the parts don't add up to the whole.
- **No refusal.** Nothing ran near a limit, and no queries ran at the same time. At low limits a query can take
  other paths.

## Known to be slower than `main`

The release gate's comparison with `main` kept 49 rows that lose in two rounds running, each with its 95 %
interval on the loss side. Three pairs are one run under two names, so they are 46 measurements. All were measured
on tree `180860fbadc7`; the figures are each side's median.

| Cause | Rows | What is slower | This release against `main` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening the store; cause not established | 12 | opening a store of 1 M statements, or of DBpedia, and the first lookups after it | 1.23 to 2.92 times; an open from cold cache 5.0 against 2.9 ms, a first lookup after a warm open 429 against 147 µs |
| The order of term ids: a bulk load numbers new terms in input order, which spreads the data a query reaches over more disk pages | 11 | a query's first run after DBpedia's store is reopened | 1.06 to 1.72 times; populations after a cold reopen 176 against 102 ms |
| The dictionary's layout for string work: texts stored in one order and numbered in another | 3 | a label prefix search, `CONTAINS`, `STRSTARTS` | 1.007 to 1.075 times; `STRSTARTS` 1.60 against 1.48 ms |
| The text search's check per match | 1 | a full-text prefix search | 85.6 against 79.5 ms (1.08) |
| Planning dearer and running cheaper, in instructions; not established as the time | 4 | small star queries on WatDiv 10 M | 15 to 16 against 12.5 to 13 µs (1.20) |
| The fixed cost per query; not established | 5 | tiny lookups, and star queries on WatDiv 100 M | 2 against 1 µs; 8 to 10.5 against 6 to 9 µs |
| The OWL 2 QL rewrite on every run, which `main` doesn't do | 2 | OWL 2 QL counts with the schema reused | 5 against 1 and 2 µs |
| The merge loop's extra passes | 8 | star queries on WatDiv 100 M, and two linear ones that may share the cause | 1.11 to 1.18 times; 166 to 169 against 148 to 153 µs |
| Not established | 1 | OWL 2 RL reasoning with `owl:sameAs` by replication | 3.83 against 3.71 s (1.035) |
| Memory the allocator keeps after freeing it, inferred | 1 | the peak memory of WatDiv 10 M | 1,147 against 1,056 MiB (1.09) |
| Not explained; it doesn't show in instruction counts | 1 | an aggregate, a score per tag | 10.54 against 10.30 ms (1.03) |

- Two rows show their second round, since in the third their intervals cross 1: a first `OPTIONAL` query after a
  cold reopen (1.09) and a warm reopen of DBpedia (1.31).
- Thirteen rows differ by a few microseconds, with a timer step of 1 µs: their ratios rest on one to four steps.
  The tiny lookup and the two OWL 2 QL counts are better read as differences, 1 to 4 µs, than as ratios.

Measured apart from that comparison:
- **A reader allowed only some graphs, under an access policy with `inferred = "supported"`,** by about 10 µs a
  read: inside a transaction on each read after an add or a remove in it, and on its first read after a commit.
  Every other read, and every reader under `inferred = "visible"` or the default `"hidden"`, pays nothing more.
- **Under compact equality, every read inside a transaction,** which scans its view's lists. The nearest figure
  measured is one such scan per read, 10 µs more (254 µs against 244); compact equality itself wasn't measured.
- **Classification of the smallest inputs**, by 1 to 5 ms, and the memory of a bulk load and of an EL
  classification: [above](#trade-offs-accepted-for-this-release).

## Not compared, and why

- **Some cases have no comparison with one baseline.** 34 declared absences (13 against the v1.5 baseline, 21
  against `main`) cover 103 cases. A case is left uncompared when that baseline's measurement build doesn't parse
  an option the case needs: the checkpoint trigger, held views, the memory limit, Turtle parsing and splitting, the
  vector graph, read graphs, recovery, the supported-inference modes and uploads; against `main` also the cache
  size, canonicalisation, clients, commits and readers, among others. Each port is planned right after v1.5; one is
  partial. The cases are in `benches/release-tradeoffs.toml`.
- **`ready` (a load plus the planner statistics' build) is compared against `main` only.** The v1.5 baseline's
  measurement tool doesn't build the statistics, so its figure would leave the build out. `load_s`, the load
  proper, is compared against both.
- **The RDF 1.2 join within 2 GB:** neither baseline completes it under that cap, which is declared. The hierarchy
  cases get 16 GB on every side instead.
- **DBLP has no query timing in this release:** the 28 GiB in-memory route fails the cap on `main` and on this
  release alike.
- **Exact live-memory peaks** are instrumentation, not a compared series.

## How the `owl2-dl` answers were checked

Generated cases, 5,000 of them with 32,368 writes and queries, ran against the store and against two OWL 2 DL
reasoners, HermiT and Openllet, as references.
- **Where a reference answered** (27,625 rows), the store's answer agrees with at least one of them, and with every
  one that answered on all but 13 rows. On 12 of those the two references disagree with each other and the store
  agrees with one of them; on one, a reference's answer differs from run to run.
- **No answer to compare:** on 1,429 rows the store declined a write as undecided, and on 3,314 no reference
  answered, mostly because one timed out after 30 s.
- **State by state:** each case's commits applied one by one, 12,104 states in 4,862 cases. Every state the store
  accepted, or refused as inconsistent, had a reference's answer, and none the store accepted is inconsistent by it,
  nor any it refused consistent.

## Not there yet

One writer at a time; no automatic failover or partitioning; a named graph isn't a reasoning boundary; no packages
on crates.io or published images yet. A primary under `reasoner.equality = "compact"` whose rules reason with
`owl:sameAs` serves no replica, and only the default repository is replicated
([replication](../ops/replication.md#limits-of-this-first-step)). What `owl2-dl` leaves undecided, and every other
limit with what to do about it: [limits and deviations](../limits.md).
