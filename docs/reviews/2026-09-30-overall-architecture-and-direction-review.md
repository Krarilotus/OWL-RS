# Build OWL-RS into a trusted contender

**Review date: 30 September 2026. Project: OWL-RS, whose current product and crates are named NRESE.**

OWL-RS now has a credible foundation for an excellent open-source, single-node triplestore: its own transactional storage engine, shared encoded execution, native SPARQL evaluation, and queryable incremental rule materialisation are substantial delivered work. The September redesign has a defensible rationale and should be preserved. However, the evidence does not yet establish complete OWL 2 RL/RDF reasoning, whole-product GraphDB/Fuseki parity, or superior performance across production workloads. The highest-priority defect is a lifecycle inconsistency: full materialisation can mark inconsistent data current, while subsequent incremental checking assumes a consistent baseline. The next priorities are an honest supported-semantic contract, correct error/configuration boundaries, usable packaged and authenticated clients, and reproducible comparisons. The strongest route to the stated ambition is to make these guarantees dependable before adding breadth; a further wholesale architectural rewrite is not justified by this review. This report separates confirmed behavior, source findings, historical measurements, and future design throughout.

## The September shift creates a more achievable product

### The original aim was broader than today's reasoning contract

The earliest committed snapshot, `fb8e270` on 25 March 2026, described gradual Fuseki replacement and an enterprise Rust semantic platform with integrated native **OWL 2 DL** reasoning. Its historical vision explicitly resisted downgrading that target through MVP shortcuts. This is evidence of the first committed direction, rather than the project's actual start date. The preserved release plan also records the complexity of the DL ambition. The historical vision can be inspected with `git show fb8e270:docs/spec/00-vision-and-scope.md`; the archived roadmap preserves its rationale. ([Original release direction](../archive/05-roadmap-and-acceptance.md), [Archive status](../archive/README.md))

March and early April then delivered a bounded implementation around that aim: reasoning telemetry (`9a246ea`, 31 March), property-chain and harness work (`d8c842e`, 1 April), a shared frontend client and CLI (`96c490e`, 1 April), local Fuseki comparison evidence (`8b35481`, 8 April), mutation orchestration (`cac6ba1`, 8 April), and coverage diagnostics (`732fec0`, 9 April). These commits establish useful accumulated work, but the bounded `rules-mvp` reasoner was not the original complete DL promise. Historical acceptance documents remain useful context when clearly labelled as historical. ([Archived bounded-era scope](../archive/05-roadmap-and-acceptance.md))

The decisive reset occurred on 25 September. Store-owned mutation orchestration, the new engine/WAL/MVCC foundation, the SPARQL adapter, and rewritten architecture/ADRs arrived through `9ea513d`, `d819447`, `e573e0e`, and `2ed5421`; `c0c68c3` moved the serving product onto the engine. The justification was concrete: v1 cloned the store on writes, had limited reasoner visibility, and brought a RocksDB/libclang deployment dependency. Owning the engine addresses those constraints while retaining established parsers and a reference evaluator. That is a sound change in mechanism, coupled with a much larger responsibility for storage correctness. ([ADR-0001](../adr/0001-own-engine-reuse-oxigraph-parsers.md))

**The changed ambition is explicit rather than accidental.** Accepted ADR-0004 makes QLever the performance reference and GraphDB the semantic/governance reference; Fuseki remains a protocol correctness reference. The current vision excludes complete OWL 2 DL and distributed clustering/high availability for now. On 26 September, decisions D9/D10 elevated ontology reasoning and state-of-the-art basics; on 27 September, D1 moved performance/reasoning ahead of governance and D11 chose shared execution. These decisions support the user's present emphasis, but they genuinely narrow the original reasoning promise from DL to a rule-based target. ([ADR-0004](../adr/0004-parity-targets-qlever-graphdb.md), [Current vision](../spec/00-vision-and-scope.md), [Recorded decisions](../ROADMAP.md))

The late-September work follows that direction. Native query execution/shared kernels (`f3c3f1d`, 27 September), reasoner IR/batch execution (`98005c4`/`6507fae`, 28 September), commit-path maintenance (`1c818c6`, 28 September), and selectable read models (`0e7f919`, 28 September) were followed by v1 removal (`7754310`, 29 September), statistics/EXPLAIN (`9d08f73`), compression/checkpoint format work (`67ad681`/`125b1d8`), and revision-keyed caching (`a6935a2`, 29 September). The assessed work therefore extends well beyond an asserted-only gate over a generic evaluator. Old reviews describing that architecture are obsolete. ([Implementation record](../ROADMAP.md), [Query dispatch](../../crates/nrese-sparql/src/query.rs), [Reasoning integration](../../crates/nrese-store/src/reasoning.rs))

### Parity must name the product, configuration, and guarantee

A useful parity contract has three separate dimensions: standards behavior, supported single-node workflows, and measured performance envelopes. Matching one does not establish the others. For example, a high-throughput RL materialiser does not establish complete DL classification, and matching a query answer count does not prove matching answers. W3C distinguishes EL, QL, and RL and gives restricted conditions for RL completeness; dataset labels such as “OWL2Bench DL” do not change the semantics of the configured rule engine. ([W3C OWL 2 profiles](https://www.w3.org/TR/owl2-profiles/))

The external comparison below is bounded to sources accessed on 30 September 2026. **GraphDB evidence is version 11.0 documentation, not a claim that 11.0 is the latest release.** The benchmark repository's GraphDB distribution version is a separate provenance issue. QLever and Oxigraph descriptions come from their official repositories as accessed on that date; these moving pages are capability references, not pinned benchmark artifacts.

| Reference | Established comparison boundary | Consequence for OWL-RS |
|---|---|---|
| Fuseki plus TDB2 | Fuseki provides HTTP query/update/Graph Store; TDB2 provides storage, single-writer/multi-reader transactions, and administrative backup/compaction. ([Fuseki](https://jena.apache.org/documentation/fuseki2/), [TDB2](https://jena.apache.org/documentation/tdb2/), [Administration](https://jena.apache.org/documentation/tdb2/tdb2_admin.html)) | Compare the deployed HTTP stack with Fuseki plus a specified dataset; compare engine guarantees with the selected TDB2 configuration. |
| Jena reasoning and validation | Built-in OWL reasoners are incomplete; Jena points complete DL requirements toward external reasoners. SHACL support includes Core and SPARQL constraints, with an explicitly configured Fuseki service. ([Inference](https://jena.apache.org/documentation/inference/), [SHACL](https://jena.apache.org/documentation/shacl/)) | Neither “OWL full” naming nor a validation endpoint proves complete reasoning or automatic validation of every commit. |
| GraphDB 11.0 | Documents materialisation, retraction, custom rules, explicit/implicit data, and profile options. Optimised rules have omissions; consistency defaults off; its QL documentation records non-conformance. ([Reasoning](https://graphdb.ontotext.com/documentation/11.0/reasoning.html)) | Pin the ruleset, consistency, equality, graph, and axiom policies before treating GraphDB as an oracle. |
| GraphDB editions | The 11.0 matrix separates Free limits and Enterprise HA/security/connectors. Free is not equivalent to an open-source database engine; current 11.0 setup requires a license file. ([Edition matrix](https://graphdb.ontotext.com/documentation/11.0/licensing.html), [Historical explicit OSS distinction](https://graphdb.ontotext.com/documentation/10.0-M3/setting-up-licenses.html), [11.0 license setup](https://graphdb.ontotext.com/documentation/11.0/set-up-your-license.html)) | Specify which single-node features are sought; Enterprise-wide parity exceeds the present HA exclusion. |
| QLever | Its official README describes updates, federation, named graphs, GSP, text/spatial search, query analysis and completion, in addition to query performance. ([Official repository](https://github.com/ad-freiburg/qlever)) | It is not a read-only target. Vendor speed claims require independent matched measurements; rule/SHACL guarantees were not established here. |
| Oxigraph and RDF4J | Oxigraph supplies reusable Rust standards components; RDF4J documents delta-informed commit SHACL validation and shape-change revalidation with explicit coverage limits. ([Oxigraph](https://github.com/oxigraph/oxigraph), [RDF4J SHACL](https://rdf4j.org/documentation/programming/shacl/)) | Reuse standards components, but retain an independent oracle; make SHACL coverage and shape-update behavior explicit. |

The coherent differentiated product is a fast, standards-oriented, durable single-node SPARQL service with transactional materialised reasoning, then a governed application stack. ResearchSpace/RDF4J/datamodel compatibility should be expressed as integration contracts over general primitives. Full DL and HA remain separately scoped future programs. If DL later becomes necessary, a revision-tagged classification/consistency adapter with complete/unsupported/unknown outcomes is preferable to silently treating RL output as DL reasoning.

## The architecture already separates the important concerns

### Seven crates implement meaningful ownership

The actual workspace has seven Rust crates, with shared edition 2024, version 0.1.0, and Apache-2.0 package metadata. The TypeScript console/CLI and separate benchmark workspace sit outside that Rust workspace. A future SHACL crate appears in architectural plans but is not a current workspace member; the shared execution crate is current even though older diagrams omit it. ([Workspace manifest](../../Cargo.toml), [Architecture](../ARCHITECTURE.md))

| Component | Current responsibility | Boundary to preserve |
|---|---|---|
| `nrese-core` | Shared report/capability vocabulary | No storage or transport algorithms |
| `nrese-engine` | Dictionary, term IDs, indexes, snapshots, transactions, WAL/checkpoints, maintenance/statistics | No SPARQL, reasoning, or HTTP policy |
| `nrese-exec` | Encoded tables, joins, grouping, graph kernels and memory budgets | No RDF decoding or domain semantics |
| `nrese-sparql` | Parsing/evaluation adapters, native planning/execution, updates and serialization | No independent commit or HTTP decisions |
| `nrese-reasoner` | Rules/IR, list-axiom compilation, batch/delta maintenance and consistency | No storage layout or transport |
| `nrese-store` | Application operations, mutation/gate ordering, revision coordination | No auth or HTTP status policy |
| `nrese-server` | HTTP, auth, deployment posture, configuration parsing, hosting | No independent data semantics |

This is implementation-level modularity: SPARQL and reasoning share execution kernels; store coordinates lower-layer services; server lacks a direct engine dependency. `nrese-exec` explicitly operates on IDs and takes resolvers for value-sensitive work. That shares expensive mechanics without forcing SPARQL semantics into reasoning or HTTP concerns into storage. There is no evidence that further universal interfaces, additional crate splitting, or a dynamic plugin system would improve current quality. Enforce the existing boundaries where specific defects cross them. ([Execution contract](../../crates/nrese-exec/src/lib.rs), [Store dependencies](../../crates/nrese-store/Cargo.toml), [Server dependencies](../../crates/nrese-server/Cargo.toml))

Reads take immutable snapshots and evaluate through the selected read model. Writes acquire the single writer, stage exact changes, compute inferred changes/check violations, claim cancellation-safe commit permission, and publish asserted and inferred layers under one revision. Dropping a transaction aborts it. Native-supported snapshot queries use native execution; unsupported algebra and transaction views retain spareval fallback. This provides an incremental optimization route without prematurely reimplementing every standards edge case. ([Engine facade](../../crates/nrese-engine/src/engine/mod.rs), [Transactions](../../crates/nrese-engine/src/engine/transaction.rs), [Mutation pipeline](../../crates/nrese-store/src/mutation/pipeline.rs), [Query dispatch](../../crates/nrese-sparql/src/query.rs))

The new engine uses dictionary-encoded IDs, immutable sorted runs with tombstones, indexed permutations, background compaction, and redo WAL/checkpoints. Canonical numeric/boolean forms can be inlined while noncanonical lexical forms preserve distinct RDF terms. Dedicated asserted/inferred stacks are a useful storage contract. Compression and packed checkpoint work already exist; old claims that every restart rebuilds the original indexes or that compression is merely future work should not be repeated. Nevertheless, these additions alone do not establish beyond-RAM scalability, crash correctness on every filesystem, or universal sublinear writes. A small schema/equality change can legitimately affect a large closure. ([Storage decision](../adr/0002-engine-storage-lsm-permutations.md), [Engine facade](../../crates/nrese-engine/src/engine/mod.rs), [Current implementation record](../ROADMAP.md))

### Setup is practical for development, incomplete as a release contract

The repository pins Rust 1.98.1 with rustfmt/clippy; release builds use fat LTO and one codegen unit. The development path is Cargo build/run plus the console's Node build/dev process. The frontend development proxy connects to the local server, while the running server resolves a chosen TOML file and environment overrides. Config path selection uses CLI or `NRESE_CONFIG_PATH`; environment values override file values, which override defaults. Ontology preload is optional rather than tied to a project-specific file. ([Toolchain](../../rust-toolchain.toml), [Build profile](../../Cargo.toml), [Setup](../../README.md), [Configuration precedence](../ops/config-reference.md), [Loader](../../crates/nrese-store/src/loader.rs))

A reviewable development walkthrough should start from a fresh directory, build the workspace, start the default service without an ontology, insert/query a small dataset, select the implemented `rdfs` or `owl2-rl` mode, compare asserted/materialised reads, then use on-disk mode and restart. That path tests something users actually need. It should be distinguished from authenticated production installation and from future ResearchSpace compatibility. The README currently contradicts itself: it says engine is outside the workspace and suggests optional libclang, while the manifest includes engine and the storage redesign removed that dependency. Its memory/native-execution description also lags recent work. ([README](../../README.md), [Manifest](../../Cargo.toml), [Dependency decision](../adr/0001-own-engine-reuse-oxigraph-parsers.md))

Three boundary improvements have concrete justification. First, **P1: preserve typed failure meaning at HTTP**. Update/Tell/GraphWrite/GraphDelete store failures are mapped broadly to 400; a commit wraps engine failures in `StoreError::Engine`, so storage/I/O failures can appear to be malformed requests. The store already supplies `is_request_error()`. Classify variants first, reserve SPARQL parse formatting for syntax failures, and expose server failures as suitable 5xx responses with request IDs. Acceptance requires syntax, RDF parse, cancellation, rejection and injected engine-error cases for each mutation kind without changing timeout/commit semantics. This changes erroneous client-visible behavior but does not require duplicating validation in handlers. This finding is source-confirmed; HTTP fault injection was not executed. ([Transport mapping, http/mutation.rs:43](../../crates/nrese-server/src/http/mutation.rs), [Typed classifier, error.rs:52](../../crates/nrese-store/src/error.rs), [Commit wrapping, pipeline.rs:151](../../crates/nrese-store/src/mutation/pipeline.rs))

Second, **P1: make unchecked writes visibly exceptional**. Public `StoreService` raw apply/update/graph/restore methods deliberately bypass gates for tests/tools/bootstrap; bulk loading bypasses gates and invalidates reasoning freshness. No present HTTP restore bypass was found: it uses the pipeline. Keep efficient maintenance paths, but move them into an explicitly named bootstrap/maintenance facade or otherwise require clearly documented preconditions. Acceptance is that every ordinary production adapter uses the validated pipeline and every unchecked operation leaves an explicit unvalidated/freshness state. The cost is embedded API migration, justified by preventing a future RDF4J or other adapter from accidentally omitting semantic gates. ([Raw API contract](../../crates/nrese-store/src/service.rs), [HTTP restore](../../crates/nrese-server/src/http/admin_dataset.rs))

Third, preserve a single publication boundary as SHACL, text, geo or vector features arrive. Compiled shapes and secondary indexes should identify the revision they describe; readers must not observe data and an index from different committed revisions. The planned reasoner-then-SHACL gate order belongs in store orchestration, while each semantic subsystem owns its own algorithms. This is an extension criterion grounded in the existing transactional model, not a claim that planned subsystems are already implemented. ([Ownership and write flow](../ARCHITECTURE.md), [SHACL decision](../adr/0005-builtin-shacl.md))

## Reasoning quality depends on lifecycle and explicit semantics

### P0: establish consistency before trusting incremental checks

**The most important reproduced defect is that materialisation freshness is treated as consistency validity.** Full `rematerialise` computes closure and violations, publishes inferences, records the ruleset as current, and returns success with a violation count. Startup ignores that count and later marks readiness. The incremental reasoner explicitly assumes its prior baseline is consistent and searches violations through newly added facts. These contracts do not fit together. ([Materialisation publication, service.rs:303](../../crates/nrese-store/src/service.rs), [Startup ignores report, main.rs:40](../../crates/nrese-server/src/main.rs), [Readiness, main.rs:66](../../crates/nrese-server/src/main.rs), [Consistent-base assumption, delta.rs:24](../../crates/nrese-reasoner/src/v2/delta.rs))

A bounded external API probe loaded disabled-mode assertions equivalent to `x a A,B; A owl:disjointWith B`, rematerialised under OWL2-RL, and performed an unrelated strict write. It reported `violations=1, marker=Some("owl2-rl")` and `unrelated strict write=Ok(Applied { revision: 2 })`. Thus this is more than a speculative startup risk. The probe used local path crates with independently resolved cached patch dependencies, not the exact root lockfile; no root-lock server restart was performed. Source tracing independently confirms the lifecycle mismatch. Retained external probe logs document execution; the repository sources above explain the issue without requiring those logs.

The fix belongs to store lifecycle policy and reasoner consistency results. Under the current rejecting contract, import/startup/rematerialisation must establish consistency before advertising a valid strict baseline. Preserve inconsistent raw data for diagnosis, but fail strict readiness or enter an explicitly named quarantine/report state. Persist validity separately from materialisation freshness. A future typed `reject/report/off` policy must be shared by normal writes, bulk validation, startup and rematerialisation; it is currently a design setting, not an implemented knob. Acceptance requires disabled import of conflicting data, enabling strict mode, no healthy strict readiness, repair/revalidation, and normal later incremental commits. Rejected/cancelled changes must preserve both layers and revision. The tradeoff is that previously accepted inconsistent installations stop being silently served as strict; explicit diagnosis and repair are necessary migration behavior.

The current freshness marker trusts the ruleset name alone. A code upgrade can change the meaning of `owl2-rl` without changing that string, so startup can skip a necessary revalidation/rematerialisation. Persist a semantic fingerprint covering the program/profile version and relevant policies, alongside revision/validity. Acceptance requires a semantic change to force rebuild/validation or a documented migration, while unchanged semantics retain the fast restart path. This is an upgrade-validity risk, not a demonstrated marker crash-atomicity defect. ([Marker handling, service.rs:85](../../crates/nrese-store/src/service.rs), [Startup skip, main.rs:36](../../crates/nrese-server/src/main.rs))

### P1: label the implemented RL/RDF slice precisely

Reasoner v2 has substantial machinery: named rule data, RDF-list compilation, bounded general property chains, keys, intersections/unions/disjointness, equality processing, batch closure and deletion maintenance. It stores queryable inferences; it is no longer the v1 IRI-only/binary-chain-only gate. However, source explicitly omits `eq-ref` and datatype Table 8; the RDFS preset is a six-rule subset. These omissions mean the mode name alone cannot communicate complete RL/RDF or RDFS entailment. W3C itself excludes RDF/RDFS axiomatic triples from the RL/RDF rule set for performance, so axiom omission alone is not the argument for non-completeness. ([Rulesets](../../crates/nrese-reasoner/src/v2/rulesets.rs), [List compiler](../../crates/nrese-reasoner/src/v2/lists.rs), [W3C RL/RDF rules](https://www.w3.org/TR/2012/REC-owl2-profiles-20121211/#Reasoning_in_OWL_2_RL_and_RDF_Graphs_using_Rules))

A second API probe accepted `x owl:differentFrom x` in a fresh OWL2-RL pipeline at revision 1. Reflexive equality would satisfy the premise of the inconsistency rule; omitting reflexivity loses this case unless another rule derives that premise. A direct consistency check or semantic reflexivity can repair it without materialising every reflexive sameAs triple. Acceptance requires self-difference rejection on fresh writes and full materialisation, including restart/lifecycle paths. Datatype value-space consistency/equality remains a separate implementation task; globally folding lexical forms would incorrectly change RDF term identity. ([Equality rules, rulesets.rs:63](../../crates/nrese-reasoner/src/v2/rulesets.rs), [W3C equality/datatype tables](https://www.w3.org/TR/2012/REC-owl2-profiles-20121211/#Reasoning_in_OWL_2_RL_and_RDF_Graphs_using_Rules))

Publish a resolved support contract: supported/omitted rule IDs, datatype map, generalized-triple boundary, graph scope, placement, consistency policy, list limits, read model, freshness and semantic fingerprint. Current full reasoning permits generalized intermediates, then persists RDF-legal facts; incremental reasoning reads persisted facts. Source acknowledges a resulting boundary for some generalized cases. Declare that boundary, test representative cases, and return deterministic unsupported/warning outcomes where appropriate. Do not turn a limited support declaration into a blanket unsoundness claim. ([Store reasoning boundary](../../crates/nrese-store/src/reasoning.rs), [Profile metadata](../../crates/nrese-reasoner/src/profile.rs))

**P1: retain ontology diagnostics through the public boundary.** The list compiler already diagnoses malformed, cyclic and oversized lists; some delta callers discard those diagnostics, and rematerialisation does not surface closure diagnostics. An operator can therefore enable an ontology whose effective semantics are silently weaker than intended. Carry typed codes, severity, axiom resource/source graph and limit context into materialisation/commit reports. Acceptance covers cyclic lists, missing rest links, oversized chains and repaired valid chains with visible deterministic diagnostics and expected consequences. Strict rejection versus permissive warnings is a product policy; the inner compiler should supply facts rather than decide HTTP behavior. ([List diagnostics](../../crates/nrese-reasoner/src/v2/lists.rs), [Discarding callers](../../crates/nrese-reasoner/src/v2/delta.rs), [Closure/report boundary](../../crates/nrese-store/src/reasoning.rs), [Rematerialisation caller](../../crates/nrese-store/src/service.rs))

### Graph scope and retraction are user-visible contracts

Current reasoning ranges over the union of default/named assertions, places inferred facts in the default graph, and suppresses an inferred triple when it is asserted in any graph. Named graphs therefore do not provide reasoning isolation or inferred provenance. This is a deliberate useful policy, not a defect by itself. SPARQL supports asserted/materialised/inferred selection, including `infer=false` and GraphDB-style pseudo-graphs; Graph Store reads use materialised data without the same selection option in the inspected path. ([Reasoning scope](../../crates/nrese-store/src/reasoning.rs), [Read-model parsing](../../crates/nrese-store/src/query_executor.rs), [Graph Store reads](../../crates/nrese-store/src/graph_store_executor.rs))

A tutorial and acceptance matrix should cover schema/instances in separate graphs, default versus named queries, duplicate support across graphs, deletion of one support, asserted/inferred reads, and cross-graph inconsistency. Make repository isolation or explicit graph-scoped reasoning an intentional feature when needed; a graph name must not be advertised as an isolation boundary today. Alternate inferred placement and source-graph reasoning remain planned. This matters directly to ResearchSpace users deciding where to put schema and data. ([Planned profile/placement](../design/reasoner-v2.md), [Roadmap packages](../ROADMAP.md))

The existing engine-level differential histories and broader reasoner tests are valuable. Extend them with equality merges/splits, cyclic multiple supports, changing blank-node lists, literal/datatype cases, schema changes, cancellations, checkpoints and reopening. Compare exact normalized inferred sets and consistency outcomes after every commit with independent recomputation; internal full recomputation validates maintenance but not the correctness of a shared incomplete ruleset. Current equality expansion is delivered, whereas persistent representative rewriting/read-time expansion is still a design. Accept future rewriting on exact answer multiplicity, delete/equality-split correctness and measured memory/materialisation/read tradeoffs, rather than assuming every workload improves. ([Pipeline differential tests](../../crates/nrese-store/tests/mutation_pipeline_tests.rs), [Reasoner tests](../../crates/nrese-reasoner/src/v2/mod.rs), [Equality implementation](../../crates/nrese-reasoner/src/v2/batch.rs))

**P1: correct narrow graph protocol discrepancies.** Source shows missing named-graph GET/HEAD returning serialized emptiness with 200 and DELETE returning 204; W3C specifies missing addressed-graph error behavior. The no-empty-graph registry is permitted, but under that choice missing CLEAR/DROP should succeed; current code errors unless SILENT. Add existence to typed store results and map it at HTTP, and correct CLEAR/DROP for the chosen storage policy. Acceptance covers missing/new/existing/emptied/default graphs across query/update/GSP methods. These are source findings, not executed HTTP reproductions. The later native-WHERE commit leaves the CLEAR/DROP branch unchanged; demanding a full graph catalog would overshoot this repair. ([Graph handlers](../../crates/nrese-server/src/http/graph_store.rs), [Graph executor](../../crates/nrese-store/src/graph_store_executor.rs), [CLEAR implementation](../../crates/nrese-sparql/src/update.rs), [W3C Graph Store](https://www.w3.org/TR/sparql11-http-rdf-update/#http-status-codes), [W3C CLEAR/DROP](https://www.w3.org/TR/sparql11-update/#clear))

## Performance progress needs stronger comparative evidence

### Historical gains are real evidence with limited scope

The repository has a multi-engine HTTP scorecard, in-process native perf-lab artifacts, and local reasoning comparisons. These constitute meaningful engineering evidence. Historical 10M end-to-end, disabled-reasoning insert measurements report in-memory p50/p99 of **216/417 microseconds** and durable **2048/3859 microseconds**. They distinguish storage microbenchmarks from full application operations; this review did not rerun them. ([Recorded baselines](../../benches/baselines/README.md))

September 27 native progression artifacts report entity-workload sum-p50 moving from 4201.626 to 254.783 ms and DBpedia from 19341.694 to 2279.256 ms, approximately 16.49x and 8.49x. Peak MiB rises from 3320 to 3656 and 16696 to 20478 respectively. Thus optimization improved these latency aggregates with a memory tradeoff. These are in-process comparisons between implementations, not HTTP competitor ratios. The newer reports were committed on 27 September, before the 29 September result cache; **the later cache does not invalidate those genuine pre-cache runs**. ([Entity baseline](../../benches/baselines/perf-lab/2026-09-27-r198-mi-lto-v3-entities-10000000.json), [Entity native result](../../benches/baselines/perf-lab/2026-09-27-xc3c-entities-10000000.json), [DBpedia baseline](../../benches/baselines/perf-lab/2026-09-27-r198-mi-lto-v3-dbpedia-core.json), [DBpedia native result](../../benches/baselines/perf-lab/2026-09-27-xc3c-dbpedia-core.json))

A local September 29 reasoning scorecard reports LUBM100 with 13,405,674 asserted and 8,726,910 inferred, v2 closure at 3.76 seconds/3560 MiB versus Nemo at 318.611 seconds/7018 MiB, and normalized instance/schema precision and recall of 1.0. These are promising **local, unrepeated observations**, not externally reproducible superiority evidence: artifacts are git-ignored and lack a precise producing machine/commit manifest. Some OWL2Bench rows lack reference/correctness fields; EL/QL-labelled datasets do not establish EL/QL completeness. Wrapped COUNT queries and best-of-four timings also omit full answer serialization. Preserve the results as development evidence while improving their provenance. ([Local large scorecard, when retained](../../benches/reasoning/results/2026-09-29-office/scorecard-large.csv), [Small scorecard, when retained](../../benches/reasoning/results/2026-09-29-office/scorecard-small.csv), [Reasoning task definitions](../design/reasoning-benchmark.md), [Comparison normalization](../../benches/reasoning/compare_inferred.py))

The 26 September competitor scorecard candidly records one load, warmup plus five query samples, unequal durability, a QLever transport floor around 44 ms, and exclusions of unsuccessful queries from aggregate latency. Its throughput mix selects each engine's own fast subset, so aggregate q/s is not identical work. It also predates the major native/reasoner changes. This is useful exploratory diagnosis, but neither its rankings nor its old bottleneck explanation should describe today's runtime without a new run. ([Scorecard](../../benches/competitors/SCORECARD.md), [Query-mix selection](../../benches/nrese-bench-harness/src/querymix.rs), [Competitor script](../../benches/competitors/scorecard.sh))

### P1: benchmark equal results and equal work

Reasoning parity must compare exact normalized closures and consistency classifications; SELECT requires solution multisets, including duplicates/unbound values, rather than sets or counts. Graph results require blank-node-aware comparison; ordering matters when required. Equal totals can hide one missing and one erroneous result. The roadmap's headline count criterion is weaker than its detailed set criterion. Keep totals as a sanity check, use false-positive/false-negative differences as the release gate, and record normalization exclusions. spareval is a useful differential oracle, but inherited spareval defects require W3C and independently implemented checks. ([Roadmap criteria](../ROADMAP.md), [Semantic target](../design/reasoner-v2.md), [W3C test harness](../../crates/nrese-sparql/tests/w3c_sparql11/main.rs))

Use a fixed preregistered common query/update mix, common deadlines/concurrency, and count failures/timeouts as outcomes. Engine-specific interactive eligibility can be a separate useful report. Pin images by digest, including QLever rather than mutable latest; record source SHA/dirty patch, both lockfiles, dataset/query hashes, filtering, ruleset/read model, cache and durability policy, compiler flags, CPU/RAM/OS/disk and container/WSL limits. Report raw samples, distributions/tails, memory/storage, restart/restore and concurrent read/write behavior. This makes relative speed claims reviewable without promising a universal winner. A relaxed acknowledgment and a durable fsync acknowledgment must never be collapsed into one write-throughput ranking. ([Current scorecard setup](../../benches/competitors/scorecard.sh), [Benchmark documentation](../../benches/competitors/README.md), [Durability policy](../../crates/nrese-engine/src/durability/mod.rs))

Result caching is a legitimate product optimization. It nevertheless measures a different task from evaluation. At the initially reviewed snapshot, perf-lab and reasoning query examples used default 64 MiB store cache plus warmups, so repeated evaluations could become serialized-result hits. ### P1: make resource limits apply to the whole service

Native execution has meaningful intermediate budgets; the documented memory option does not reach spareval fallback. Per-query default allowance is 4 GiB, which does not itself cap aggregate concurrent memory. Cached output, auxiliary structures, fallback evaluation and reasoning need an explicit accounting/admission contract. This is a source-confirmed incomplete guarantee, not a measured OOM or proof that every native allocation escapes accounting. Begin with aggregate admission and explicit path limitations; add cooperative charging where feasible. Acceptance is bounded peak behavior for native/fallback queries, concurrent clients, expansive closures and cache pressure, plus clear reject/cancel outcomes. ([Query option/fallback](../../crates/nrese-sparql/src/query.rs), [Budget](../../crates/nrese-exec/src/budget.rs), [Server default](../../crates/nrese-server/src/policy.rs))

The mutation ticket correctly prevents a cancelled write from publishing, and HTTP waits for the real result once commit has started. Preserve that guarantee. However, cancellation does not reach incremental materialisation's inner executor, so a timed-out request can still hold the single writer while expensive reasoning runs. Carry cancellation/work budgets into rounds, joins, list grounding and equality expansion while retaining the final atomic guard. Acceptance requires cancellation of a large reasoning update, measured bounded writer release, unchanged asserted/inferred state and revision, and a prompt following small update. Administrative jobs need visible progress and duration. This improves availability without weakening transaction safety. ([Timeout adapter](../../crates/nrese-server/src/http/mutation.rs), [Pipeline cancellation](../../crates/nrese-store/src/mutation/pipeline.rs), [Reasoning call boundary](../../crates/nrese-store/src/reasoning.rs))

Optimization priorities after these gates should follow profiles of matched workloads. Equality rewriting, disk-backed runs, parallelism, group commit and specialized indexes each exchange CPU, memory, write tails or durability. Do not infer their priority from a historical bottleneck or demand every threshold become configurable. Publish absolute user budgets alongside comparative ratios so release success remains meaningful when competing systems improve.

## Conclusion

The defining opportunity is to combine fast encoded execution with semantic and operational outcomes that users can inspect and trust. The architecture already makes that combination plausible; the next competitive advantage is eliminating ambiguity about validity, supported inference, failure meaning, configuration, and deployment. A smaller precisely verified envelope earns more credibility than broader parity language backed by counts or historical timing.

The project should preserve its ambitious performance/reasoning direction while treating clean installation, strict lifecycle transitions and independently reproducible results as product milestones. Those requirements connect modularity to measurable user value and make future governance/search features safer to add. Once these gates are met, claims of leadership can name the workload and guarantees they actually win, providing a robust basis for an outstanding open-source triplestore.
